# Notes for agents

- The owner's rule: **nothing new to buy**. Design around the parts on his machine (2020/2040
  frame, 8 mm rods on SK8s, SC8UU blocks, KP08s, NEMA 17, a closed HTD 8M 20 mm belt, AS5600 or a 600 P/R
  optical encoder, his own driver and controller) and plain M3/M4/M5 screws with 20-series
  T-nuts. Everything else is printed. `BOM.md` lists it all.
- His photos are in `git_ignore_this/` and stay private: the folder is in `.gitignore`; never
  commit or publish them, or any picture taken from them.
- The frame's length and width, the rods' spacing and the belt come from his measuring photos;
  the belt's 175 teeth from his own count (it is stamped 1400 mm), the frame's 319 mm height from
  his tape (`REPORT.md`, M5, O1, O2). If he re-measures anything, put it in `scripts/params.py`
  and run `python scripts/build_all.py`. Every other dimension follows from them. Don't count
  teeth from a photo: one read 180 for this belt (M5).
- Parts print without supports: the gate's `overhang` check slices each print-ready part and fails
  on anything that starts in mid-air, reaches more than 3 mm out held on one side, or bridges more
  than 34 mm. Fix the shape (45° gussets, chamfers) or the print orientation; don't turn supports on.
- `3mf/to_print_kobra_s1.3mf` is his print list: `scripts/make_to_print.py` leaves out the parts
  he keeps (`HAVE`), puts parts he may not need on OPTION beds (`MAYBE`: his own idler pulley may
  replace the printed one) and takes one sensor's parts (`SENSOR`). When he says what he has, edit
  those and rebuild; the gate fails when the project is stale. It holds both materials (filament 1
  PLA, 2 PETG, one material per bed, PETG beds first); `--verify` checks each bed against the same
  bed sliced with its own material's presets, and the gate audits the file (`audit()`).
- After any change to `scripts/` or `slicer_profiles/`, run `python scripts/build_all.py`
  (rebuild, re-slice, re-simulate, verify; `--no-slice` without the slicer).
  `python scripts/check_parts.py` must pass before anything is printed; CI runs it on every push.
- Generated, never edit by hand: `parts/` (STLs, `assembly.json`, `sim.json`, the STEP), `3mf/`,
  `gcode/`, `BOM.md`, `viewer/manifest.json`, `renders/`. In `tracker.md` edit only the Status
  column. The gate fails when any of them is stale.
- The belt is the point of the redesign: pulleys and clamp in one plane (y = 0), both axles at one
  height, flanges on both sides, a toothed clamp, a tensioner that also squares the idler
  (`DESIGN.md`, "How the belt runs now"). The gate's `belt` check holds that; keep it passing
  rather than loosening it.
- The pendulum must turn a full circle at every cart position (swing-up needs it): the gate's
  `pendulum` check sweeps it. Anything added to the cart's front must stay out of that disc.
- Control numbers come from `scripts/sim.py`, which weighs the sliced parts. After changing a
  pendulum or carriage part, `build_all.py` re-runs it; update `DESIGN.md`'s Control section if
  the gains or times move.
- The controller exists twice, line for line: `sim.py`'s `Controller` and
  `firmware/pendulum/controller.h`. Change both together; the gate's `firmware` check replays
  simulated runs through the C++ (g++/clang++, or WSL's g++ on Windows; CI has g++) and fails on
  any difference in command or mode. `firmware/pendulum/config.h` is generated by
  `make_firmware.py` from `params.py` and `sim.json`: never edit it. Pins and the two direction
  settings live at the top of `pendulum.ino`.
- New controller numbers: `python scripts/tune_controller.py` (all cores, minutes) searches over
  `sim.ROBUST` (model error x corners, plus 24 random runs). Before pasting candidates into
  `CANDIDATES`, check them on scenarios they were not tuned on: the best on the tuning runs has
  failed 1 in 5 unseen ones (REPORT.md, P3-P6). The bounds (delay, pivot friction, upright error,
  drive current, friction) are in `params.py`; each one is a premortem finding, so loosen none
  without the check on the machine that justifies it.
- "Balanced" is not "still": a candidate that passes every run can still hunt. The pivot's
  stick-slip sets the hunting (`sim.hunting`, REPORT.md P16): an observer and friction compensation
  barely move it, but stiff angle and speed gains (large q on v and theta, small on x) delay its
  onset, so `tune_controller.py` ranks passing candidates by it. The rest is the pivot
  (`PIVOT_FRICTION_GOAL`, the firmware's `f` test, whose estimator `PivotTest` the C++ test checks on
  simulated swings). The gate bounds the hunting at the goal: keep it passing rather than raising
  `sim.HUNT_LIMIT`.
- The machine's numbers come from the owner's photos and measurements (REPORT.md, M1-M6, O1, O2):
  `FRAME_H` 319 (his tape), `BELT_TEETH` 175 (his count). `BELT_TEETH`
  sets the pulleys' spacing, and the travel stops follow it.
- Firmware compile check (not in CI): arduino-cli with the `arduino:avr` and `esp32:esp32` cores and
  FastAccelStepper; it builds for `arduino:avr:uno`, `arduino:avr:nano` and `esp32:esp32:esp32`
  with both sensors (`USE_ENCODER` 0 and 1) and no warnings in its own files. The Nano (old
  bootloader) build with the AS5600 uses 98 % of its flash: keep new firmware text short.
- Renders: after a geometry change, `python scripts/capture.py` (headless Edge, a few minutes)
  re-renders `renders/*.png` and the README's GIFs; `--gif` redoes only the animations. Check a
  GIF has more than one frame: Pillow merges identical frames into one.
- When a finding is fixed or a new one is found, update `REPORT.md` (status table and text) in
  the same commit.
- CI runs the gate on Linux; it was built on Windows. Anything the gate recomputes and compares
  exactly (plate layouts and their stamps, the tracker, the manifest) must come out the same on
  both: round floats before comparing or hashing, and break exact ties (round parts turned 45°
  or 135°) by a fixed preference, not by float noise (`plates.py`, `_rot`). The gate prints a
  GitHub error annotation for each failed check, readable without the run's log.
- Pushing to GitHub over HTTPS: very large packs get reset on this network. Push in commits of
  about 20 MB or less (the G-code folders are the large part).
